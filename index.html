Executive summary

HR software has learned to act. The evidence that it acts well has not caught up. AI arrived in a recruiting market already under strain. Between 2022 and 2025, applications per role more than doubled while recruiters per company fell by more than half.

The value problem is not specific to HR. 88% of HR leaders told Gartner their organizations had not seen significant value from AI tools in general. BCG's 2026 AI at Work study found that organizations redesigning whole workflows got more value than those adding tools. If you are planning AI adoption across your whole organization, read the BCG report in full.

Several things are working, and they deserve more attention than they get. Forecasting shift demand is mature and well supported, and group-level resignation trends are usable. In core HR, asking a question in plain words now works, often with general AI tools. Performance tools make feedback more frequent, and being ignored looks worse for people than being criticized. AI coaching helped people reach their own goals in a randomized trial. People who move or are promoted internally tend to stay longer. HR service agents handle routine questions at volume and pass sensitive cases to a person.

Two findings about pay are promising, and less settled. On one global remote-work platform, workers in lower-income countries earned several times local income per person. Earned wage access is associated with lower turnover. The evidence behind each finding in this summary differs, and the report labels every claim.

Recruiting has the best evidence and the sharpest risks. The structured interview, the best-evidenced hiring method, has become practical to run at scale. What a screening tool is tuned for decides who gets through. In one large hiring study, 9.4% of the applicants the firm's recruiters picked for interview were Black or Hispanic. One algorithm cut that to 4.2%. A differently tuned one raised it to 24.3%. Both algorithms raised the predicted hiring rate above the recruiters' own picks, so the more diverse one gave up nothing on that measure.

What the software is told to optimize decides what it does. Scheduling software tuned only for labor cost produces efficient schedules nobody can plan a life around. The same software can let workers swap and claim shifts themselves. Ask what a tool was tuned to maximize, and who chose that.

Executive summary, continued

AI is now screening AI. Language models evaluating resumes prefer resumes written by language models. At the same time, in one large sample of real resumes, about 1% carried hidden instructions aimed at AI screeners.

Many of the numbers you are shown were measured by the seller, and that is not a problem in itself. You do not need a randomized trial to buy software. You do need to know what was measured, how, and against what. One widely quoted return on investment describes a modeled composite retailer, not a measured customer. One time-to-hire claim is compared with an industry average for which I found no source.

Agents act, mostly with permission. Agents now schedule interviews, run first-round screens, close HR cases and check payroll. Nearly every documented design stops for a person to approve. At several major vendors, announcements run ahead of availability.

The bill arrives before the saving. Agent pricing is moving to per action, so usage becomes the multiplier. The cost lands on the software budget at once. The saving only becomes money if you need fewer people or hire fewer.

The law is moving in both directions. In the EU, inferring emotion from a face or voice at work has been prohibited since February 2025. Duties for high-risk HR systems start in December 2027. Draft guidance says a human sign-off does not take a tool out of scope. In the US, federal guidance on AI in hiring was withdrawn in 2025, while states and courts are adding rules.

"AI" and "predictive" can each mean many different things sold under one word. So the strongest question for a vendor is not whether the AI works. It is what exactly it does, what it was built to maximize, and what happens when it is wrong. Part 1 explains what gets called AI and where the money sits. Why this exists

I spent eight years in HR, and sat through a lot of software demonstrations. The slides were always confident. "Studies show." "Companies see." "Up to 80%." And almost never checkable.

I also worked on an AI product that succeeded, before the current wave. That is why the hype is easy to recognize now, and why it is clear how little of it rests on evidence.

So I checked. This is the review I would want before signing anything.

Two questions, not one

Before you buy, ask two separate things.

Does it work?

Technology maturity: does the software reliably do what it claims?

Does it deliver?

Evidence strength: is there independent proof it produces the outcome you are paying for?

A product can be mature with no outcome evidence at all. That gap is where most overselling happens. Wellbeing apps show it clearly. The technology works as designed. The people using them report no better wellbeing.

01A hiring market under strain

AI did not arrive in a calm market. On Greenhouse's own platform data, applications per open role rose from 116 to 244 between 2022 and 2025. Applications per recruiter rose from 146 to 746. Recruiters per company fell from 10.43 to 4.62. Vendor-reported

Few recruiters can read 244 applications a role carefully. So both sides reached for the same tool. Candidates use AI to apply to more roles. Employers use AI to filter harder. The pile grows again.

In Greenhouse's survey of 2,900 job seekers in four countries, only 8% said AI makes hiring fairer. Vendor-reported

02The value problem is not only HR's

88% of HR leaders told Gartner their organizations had not seen significant business value from AI tools. Reported That is about AI tools in general, not HR software alone.

BCG's 2026 AI at Work study points to one reason. Organizations that redesigned whole workflows got more value than those that only added tools. More than two-thirds of regular AI users reported higher job satisfaction. 41% of all respondents reported more mental strain. Reported

04Two pay findings, promising and less settled

Remote pay. Economists studying one global remote-work platform found remote pay far more equal across countries than conventional pay. Average earnings were around $18,667. That is six to nine times income per person in countries like India, Pakistan and the Philippines. Reported It is also a fraction of what the same role pays in a Western market. A higher ceiling, not equality.

Earned wage access. A study of 51,543 workers at two large Mexican companies found users were 8% to 12% less likely to leave. Among lower-ranked staff it was about 20%. Reported The authors call it an association, not a cause, and nobody has taken it further. If someone draws early every month, the tool may be managing low pay rather than solving it.

05Recruiting: the best evidence and the sharpest risks

The structured interview is the best-evidenced idea in the report. It predicts job performance at a validity of .42, against .19 for the unstructured interview most organizations run. Verified Software now removes the two old obstacles: preparing the questions, and writing everything down.

The .42 is an average. The authors expect 80% of structured interviews to land between .18 and .66. Verified The label alone guarantees nothing.

Screening cuts both ways. On a Fortune 500 test set of 39,947 applicants, recruiters picked 9.4% Black or Hispanic applicants for interview. One algorithm cut that to 4.2%. A model built to explore raised it to 24.3%. Both raised predicted hiring rates. Verified

And a process change is not an outcome change. Across about 11,300 roles where degree requirements were removed, the net change in who got hired was 0.14%. Reported

06What the software is told to optimize

Scheduling shows the same pattern as screening. Tuned only for labor cost, it produces efficient schedules nobody can plan a life around. That is the problem you bought the software to fix.

Tuned differently, the same software gives workers control. In a commissioned model of a composite retailer, 90% of schedule changes became employee-initiated. Open shift claims rose from 34% to 58%, and absenteeism fell 19%. Vendor-reported

The technology does not choose. Someone sets the objective. Ask what a tool was tuned to maximize, and who chose that.

07AI is now screening AI

Language models evaluating CVs prefer CVs written by language models. Across 24 occupations, candidates using the same model as the evaluator were 23% to 60% more likely to be shortlisted than equally qualified people who wrote their own. Verified

The screeners are targets too. About 1% of nearly 200,000 real resumes carried hidden instructions aimed at AI screeners. Verified

Names matter as well. An audit of CV-ranking models, over more than 500 resumes, found they favored one demographic group on the name alone in the great majority of comparisons. Verified

08Numbers measured by the seller

Seller-measured numbers are not a problem in themselves. You do not need a randomized trial to buy software. You do need to know what was measured, how, and against what.

A 1,340% return on investment over three years comes from a commissioned study of a modeled composite retailer, with 15,000 employees across 700 locations. Not a real customer. Vendor-reported

A time to hire of 8.7 days is set against an "industry average" of 36 days. No source is given for the 36. Vendor-reported

Ask what the comparison is before you argue about the headline.

09Agents act, mostly with permission

Agents now schedule interviews, run first-round screens, close routine HR cases and check payroll. Workday alone reports more than 5,500 customers using at least one of its agents, across HR and finance. Vendor-reported

Nearly every documented design stops for a person to approve. So human review has not gone. It has moved later, behind a shortlist a machine already chose.

Announced is not shipped. Workday announced six new HR agents in September 2025. By March 2026, none was generally available. Verified

I found no peer-reviewed study of outcomes from a deployed HR agent. Unverified Among large US companies already using agentic AI, 49% said their governance had not been updated for it. Reported

10The bill arrives before the saving

Agent pricing in HR is moving to per action. Recruiting vendors charge overage of about $2.50 to $5 per AI interview. Workday meters its self-service agent at one credit to answer a question and five to complete a task. Verified

So employee usage becomes the multiplier you are signing. The cost lands on the software budget at once. The saving becomes money only if you need fewer people, or hire fewer. A business case should show how that happens.

Set a usage baseline before renewal, not after.

11The law is moving in both directions

In the EU, it is tightening.

Since 2 February 2025Inferring emotion from a face or voice at work is banned.
Since 2 August 2026People must be told when they are dealing with an AI system, such as a hiring chatbot.
From 2 December 2027Full duties for high-risk HR systems, from recruitment to performance monitoring.

Penalties for prohibited uses reach €35m or 7% of global turnover. Draft guidance says a human sign-off alone does not take a tool out of scope. Verified

In the US, it is loosening at the top. Federal guidance on AI in hiring was withdrawn in January 2025. Reported States and courts are adding rules instead. In Mobley v. Workday, the court let a claim against the software vendor itself proceed in July 2024. A hearing on full class certification is set for March 2027. Verified

12"AI" and "predictive" mean many things

Group forecasts work. Forecasting shift demand or group-level resignation trends is well supported. A wrong forecast costs a planning assumption.

Predictions about named individuals mostly do not. A 97.7% accuracy figure for predicting who will resign was measured on a fictional dataset of 1,470 rows. Verified Burnout prediction scored 0.70 on a scale where 0.5 is a coin toss, against 0.93 for other health outcomes. Verified

The word "AI" hides as much. The first automated hiring discrimination case the US EEOC settled involved a simple age cutoff. Verified

So ask what exactly it does, what it was built to maximize, and what happens when it is wrong.
